Key Distinctions:

  • Take-home pay / Disposable Income: Money left after tax and mandatory deductions
  • Discretionary Income: Money left after paying for housing, food, necessities (ranges from <5% for under 25 to >40% for elderly)

Cash Outflows: Fixed vs Variable Expenses

Fixed Expenses

Payments that do NOT vary month to month

  • • Rent / Mortgage payments
  • • Installment loan payments
  • • Internet service charges
  • • Insurance premiums

Variable Expenses

Flexible payments that CHANGE monthly

  • • Food (groceries & dining out)
  • • Clothing & Personal care
  • • Utilities (electricity, water)
  • • Recreation & Medical expenses
  • • Gifts and donations

Positive Cash Flow (Surplus):
Income > Outflows → Source of savings, investment, or debt payment
Negative Cash Flow (Deficit):
Income < Outflows → Financed by savings withdrawal or borrowing

Budgeting: 7 Steps to Set Up a Budget

1

Set Financial Goals

Foundation for financial direction. Plans for spending, saving, and investing.

2

Estimate Income

Include only money you are SURE to receive. Estimate on the LOW side to avoid overspending.

3

Budget Emergency Fund

3-6 months of expenses. Planning for unexpected: illness, redundancy.

4

Budget Fixed Expenses

Rent, loan payments, insurance - predictable monthly costs.

5

Budget Variable Expenses

Food, clothing, utilities, recreation - flexible spending.

6-7

Record & Review

Track spending amounts and review patterns regularly. Use smartphone apps!

Four Financial Ratios

Ratio Formula Interpretation Target
Debt Ratio Liabilities ÷ Net Worth Shows relationship between debt and net worth LOW is best
Current Ratio Liquid Assets ÷ Current Liabilities Cash available to pay bills HIGH is desirable
Liquidity Ratio Liquid Assets ÷ Monthly Expenses Number of months expenses can be paid in emergency HIGH is desirable
Debt-Payment Ratio Monthly Credit Payments ÷ Disposable Income Portion of earnings for debt payment Monitor carefully

Chapter Summary

  • • Financial ratios provide guidelines for measuring changes in financial situation
  • • Liquidity ratio = Liquid Assets ÷ Monthly Expenses (indicates months of emergency coverage)
  • • Budgeting ensures personal expenditure does not exceed income
  • • Discretionary income varies by age: <5% (under 25) to >40% (elderly)
  • • Use smartphone apps to track expenses (especially cash transactions)
